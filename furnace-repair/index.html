A modern furnace runs a strict startup sequence, and knowing where the sequence breaks is most of the diagnosis. The thermostat calls, the inducer motor spins up to prove draft through a pressure switch, the hot surface igniter glows, the gas valve opens, the burners light, the flame sensor confirms fire, and only then does the blower deliver heat to the house. In Edina, MN, the failures cluster at predictable points: a flame sensor coated in oxide that can no longer prove the flame, a hot surface igniter cracked from years of thermal cycling, a pressure switch that will not close because the inducer is weak or the condensate trap on a high-efficiency model has clogged, or a control board with a burned relay. Each one stops the sequence cold, and each one mimics the others from the hallway.

Our experienced technicians diagnose a furnace the way it operates, in order, measuring each stage as it happens. That means igniter resistance, microamp flame signal, pressure switch operation under actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor condition. The safety checks come standard on a gas appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak testing on the gas connections, and flue draft verification, since a hazard is not visible at the thermostat. We finish by checking temperature rise against the data plate, making sure the burner and blower are matched the way the manufacturer designed them to.

Edina HVAC services natural gas, propane, and electric furnaces from all major brands, from standard 80 percent units to condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drain lines, and dual pressure switches. Our trucks carry the parts that actually fail, such as igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors, so most homes have heat again before we leave. Warning Signs for Furnace Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

  • A bang or boom when the burners ignite late
  • Grinding or high-pitched squeals from the blower
  • Frequent on-off cycling that never reaches the thermostat setting
  • The furnace clicks and glows but the burners never light
  • Air moving through the vents that never gets warm
  • A flame that burns soft and yellow rather than steady blue
  • Repeated shutdowns just seconds after the burners light
  • Rust streaks, soot, or water around the cabinet or flue

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

The most serious furnace problem is usually hidden. A cracked or corroded heat exchanger can let combustion gases into the airstream, and nothing about it is visible from the outside of the cabinet. Delayed ignition makes things worse, because each boom at light-off puts more stress on that metal. Add high voltage, a gas valve, and a flue to the same box, and the margin for error shrinks. Diagnosis is full of traps too. A high limit that keeps tripping could be a bad switch, or it could be doing exactly its job because a filter is clogged or the blower is dying, and replacing a good switch only hides the real fault.

Our techs look at every symptom in the context of the whole furnace. We measure flame signal instead of assuming it, which keeps us from replacing a healthy sensor while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve goes unnoticed. Draft is confirmed at the pressure switch and at the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, so airflow issues are caught before they cut years off the heat exchanger. The gas side is leak-tested with instruments, never with a quick sniff. The result is a furnace proven safe over a full cycle, plus an honest opinion if the unit is getting close to the point where replacing it beats repairing it again.

How to Prevent Furnace Repair Problems

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Give the furnace room to breathe, since a crowded utility space starves the burners of air
  • Store laundry supplies, especially chlorine products, well away from the furnace closet
  • If you have a high-efficiency furnace, check that the condensate drain is flowing freely
  • Pay attention when the burners light, since a boom instead of a soft whoosh calls for service
  • Replace the filter every month while the furnace is working hard, since it costs less than any other part
  • Have the furnace checked every autumn, while a weak igniter or fouled sensor is still an easy fix
  • Test your CO detectors monthly and make sure each floor has one
